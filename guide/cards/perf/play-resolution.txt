# Draw play at fewer pixels than its stage, with pixelRatio

`play({ width, height, pixelRatio })` draws `pixelRatio` render pixels for each stage unit; layout, input, `game.pointer` and semantic bounds stay in stage units. A 1280 × 720 stage at `pixelRatio: 0.75` draws a canvas of 960 × 540 pixels, which the page shows at its own size.

```js
const game = await play({ width: 1280, height: 720, pixelRatio: 0.75, setup, update });
// game.canvas is 960 x 540; everything the game places is still in units of 1280 x 720.
```

Measure the empty stage first; the cost there is the floor for everything drawn on it. In Taiwan Sixteen-Tile Mahjong at engine `b5628eb`, before play had the option, an empty 1280 × 720 stage ran at 33.4 ms p95 in headless Chromium with SwiftShader, and the game drew a 960 × 540 play under a scaled root to reach 16.8 ms. At engine `2e53f45`, on another run, an empty 1280 × 720 stage was 16.8 ms p95 with and without `pixelRatio: 0.75`: lower the ratio when fill is what costs, which a measurement shows.

Rules:

- Lower `pixelRatio` before cutting what the game draws, and keep text large enough to read at the lower ratio: words drawn at 0.75 are softer.
- Do not scale `game.world` or a root sprite to fake a smaller stage any more; the old workaround moved every play-drawn effect off its place.
- Unless given, the ratio is the screen's `devicePixelRatio`, at most 2: on a phone, `pixelRatio: 1` already draws a quarter of the pixels of the default.

Learnt from Taiwan Sixteen-Tile Mahjong (ENGINE-NOTES 3); `pixelRatio` came with engine `e7184a8`.
