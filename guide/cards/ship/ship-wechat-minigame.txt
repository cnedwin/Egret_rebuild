# A game shipped as a WeChat mini game

A browser game becomes a mini game unchanged through `tools/wechat/export-wechat.mjs`, and runs first in the stand-in of `tools/wechat/check-minigame.mjs` (see `knowledge-base/en/docs/wechat-minigame.md`). What the exporter cannot change is what the game must be written for:

- **One canvas, no page.** A mini game has no HTML over the canvas. Menus, dialogue, text and buttons must be drawn by the engine (`game.text`, `game.button`, `game.menu`, `game.say`); HTML overlays are not shown. CSS backgrounds under the stage are painted by the adapter; nothing else of the page is.
- **Touch only.** No keyboard, no hover, no mouse wheel. Every action needs a tap, a drag or `game.touchStick`.
- **Size.** 4 MB for the main package and 30 MB in all. Keep the first scene's pictures in the main package; larger art and sound wait for WeChat subpackages or a download after start. Write WebP pictures again with `--playwright`.
- **Portrait and quick.** Most players open it from a chat and hold the phone upright. Choose the orientation at export; reach the first action in seconds.
- **WebGL2.** iOS needs high-performance mode enabled for the AppID. WebGPU is not there.
- **Its own services.** Login, sharing, rankings, payments and ads are `wx` calls the game makes itself, guarded so the browser build still runs. The adapter supplies none of them.
- **Review.** The owner uploads with the AppID and upload key and submits it on the platform: category, age rating, and for a paid or advertised game in mainland China the licence it requires.

Before shipping: the stand-in check, then WeChat DevTools, then a preview on an Android phone and an iPhone.
