# Hanging weights on rope joints, cut with removeJoint

Mechanism City hangs crates and stones from ropes that the player shoots through. Before the 2D world had joints, a hanging weight was a kinematic body painted swinging by hand, removed and made again as a dynamic body when its rope was cut. Both halves were wrong in play: nothing could knock it, and the remade body lost the speed it had. With joints the weight is one dynamic body from the start:

```js
import { createPhysicsWorld } from '@egret/engine/physics2d';

const world = createPhysicsWorld({ gravity: { x: 0, y: 900 }, fixedStep: 1 / 120 });
const weight = world.addBody({ kind: 'dynamic', shape: { kind: 'box', width: 40, height: 40 }, x: 300, y: 200 });
// Tie the top of the weight (anchorA, in its own frame) to a fixed point; it swings when struck.
const rope = world.addJoint({ kind: 'rope', a: weight, point: { x: 300, y: 80 }, anchorA: { x: 0, y: -20 } });
// The shot crosses the line from the point to the top of the weight: cut it.
world.removeJoint(rope);
```

Rules:

- A rope holds at most its length and goes slack when pushed closer; `kind: 'distance'` holds the length both ways, for a rod.
- Cut by removing the joint, never by removing and remaking the body: the weight keeps its speed and its swing.
- A body held up until something happens (a crate on a trapdoor, a ball in a clamp) starts `static` or `kinematic` and becomes `dynamic` with `body.setKind('dynamic')`.
- Contact listeners may add and remove joints; the change acts from the next step. Calling `world.step` inside a listener fails with `PHYSICS_STEP_REENTRANT`. Mechanism City still queues breaks, cuts and unlocks and runs them after the step, so they happen in one fixed order.
- The 2D world has no turning hinge or torque yet: a seesaw is a kinematic plank whose angle the rules set.
- Test the cut in Node: step a level with a seed, cut, and check the weight falls from where it swung.
