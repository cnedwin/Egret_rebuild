# Open-source collaboration and public communication policy

English | [简体中文](开源协作与公开表达规范.md)

Version: 1.0 · Knowledge base: 0.9.0 · October 8, 2026.

Egret uses public collaboration from early development. Code, product design, technical approaches, research materials, decision evidence, and verification records form the project together and evolve with implementation. R018 confirms this direction. The remote repository, code/documentation licenses, and maintainer information are registered separately in publication configuration.

## Communication with the community

Documents center on questions developers need to understand: target scenarios, how an approach works, why it was chosen, available evidence, and how contributors can participate. Use clear, professional, equal language, explaining specifically what problems Egret's data organization, interfaces, lifecycle, and engineering tools solve.

Innovation claims correspond to reviewable designs or results. Performance, loading, power, and compatibility conclusions include versions, content, devices, and measurement conditions. Unmeasured goals remain hypotheses awaiting verification. Explain prototypes, reference experiments, and formal product capabilities separately so developers can judge applicability.

Describe mechanisms and tradeoffs of other engines and open-source projects using public materials and applicable versions, and discuss design choices against Egret's goals. Introduce repaired problems through historical records; retain limits on version differences and inferences. Comparisons focus on technical conditions and consistent measurement, without speculating about team capability, commercial motives, or willingness to maintain.

## Sources and acknowledgements

Place research citations near related conclusions, favoring official documentation, standards, papers, and fixed-version source. [Acknowledgements](../ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS.en.md) provide a project index. Register research references, experimental execution dependencies, product dependencies, and development tools separately to clarify implementation attribution at each layer.

Egret's core is designed and implemented from its own requirements and specifications. When integrating general infrastructure as explicit dependencies, retain project identity, versions, licenses, copyright notices, and modification records. Adapters, examples, and cited materials each state their sources. See the [implementation and dependency policy](独立实现与第三方依赖规范.en.md).

Public articles primarily explain Egret's design and verification results. Source records and review processes use formal engineering language; internal discussions, provisional wording, and tool prompts should not become product narratives verbatim. Edited descriptions still accurately represent actual dependencies and verification status.

## Public design records

Organize design processes into records suitable for continuing discussion: problems and goals, candidates, adoption rationale, constraints and compatibility needs, verification or counterexamples, results, and next steps. RFCs, ADRs, experiment reports, and changelogs reference each other and retain design evolution and significant objections. Contributions disclose AI assistance scope and review responsibility.

Failure records help improve designs and should retain reproduction conditions, effects, and revision results. Temporary workbench scheduling, verbatim private chats, internal model traces, and tool-environment details are not community design materials. Curate valuable discussion into the engineering records described above.

## Material and publication boundaries

Public directories contain only curated code, documentation, research results, and shareable samples. Personal contact details, sensitive business information, unauthorized source/assets, original engineering packages, credentials, signatures, and private SDK configuration remain outside release scope. Describe historical experience through product and technical facts; record historical cooperation and current platform support separately.

Edits or omissions retain original material identity and modification type. For evidence reports, preserve results, counts, versions, source digests, and technical conclusions; public copies explain editing scope. Original materials are retained according to permissions. Edited copies must not pose as unprocessed original logs.

## Public-content checks for every contribution

- Describe the problem, change, basis, verification results, and applicable scope.
- Check source links, dependency identity, copyright notices, and sample permissions.
- Check for private information, local paths, internal scheduling, and business information.
- Reconcile implementation status across code, documentation, and registries; retain failures and remaining issues.
- Follow this project's naming and interface conventions and keep discussions clear and friendly.

These rules apply to READMEs, code comments, documentation, Issues, PRs, RFCs, ADRs, and release notes. Changes to public scope or licenses update publication configuration. Engineering quality and product acceptance continue to be assessed against actual evidence.
